Ethics for RAIDops: AI Ethical Governance Framework

Engineers cannot individually absorb every legal, ethical, policy, human-factors, and geopolitical obligation. Responsible AI requires an organizational architecture that places the right expertise and authority around development teams.

Columbia University2025Completed academic framework · collaborative research
Result / value signalGovernance designed as organizational infrastructure, not project-team burden

Context

AI delivery teams are routinely asked to resolve complex ethical and governance questions beyond their professional mandate, while fragmented review structures create ambiguity, delay, and inconsistent accountability.

Mandate

Design a practical governance and knowledge system that could surface ethical risk early, distribute expert responsibility, preserve traceable decisions, and support delivery rather than merely audit it.

What I led

  • Defined a layered governance model spanning executive ethics panels, centers of excellence, cross-functional review boards, and embedded subject-matter experts.
  • Connected escalation paths, review responsibilities, documentation, and organizational learning into one operating framework.
  • Designed knowledge-management mechanisms to address ethical risk, recurring questions, knowledge gaps, and institutional memory.
  • Clarified how accountability should be shared without shifting specialized legal or policy judgment onto engineering teams.

Outcomes

  • Produced a governance architecture with explicit oversight, expertise, and escalation responsibilities.
  • Created a repeatable knowledge system for improving transparency and decision traceability.
  • Reframed responsible AI as a cross-organizational operating capability rather than a late-stage compliance gate.

Leadership signal

Shows the ability to translate normative principles into roles, forums, knowledge flows, and decision mechanisms that executives and delivery teams can actually operate.
