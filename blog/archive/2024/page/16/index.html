    New USPTO Guidance Sheds Light on Human Roles in AI-Assisted Inventions The United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) has underscored the importance of “human contributions” in the analysis of inventorship, particularly in the arena of AI-assisted inventions, according to recent guidance. While recognizing that AI-assisted inventions are not categorically unpatentable, the USPTO emphasizes in its guidance that the assessment of inventorship should revolve around human inputs. This stance reflects a commitment to reward and incentivize the ingenuity of human inventors in the AI realm. Identifying human contributions in AI-assisted inventions can be challenging, as there is no definitive test. However, the USPTO’s guidelines lay out several integral principles to aid in this process. It notes that a person’s utilization of AI in the invention process does not disqualify them as an inventor, but simply presenting a problem to an AI system might not suffice for inventorship. With its new guidance, USPTO navigates the intricate landscape of human inventorship in the age of AI, offering a more unambiguous path to patentability. The agency will now seek public comments on the guidance over the next three months. For more insights into the USPTO’s stance on human roles in AI-assisted inventions, please refer to the original FedScoop article.
