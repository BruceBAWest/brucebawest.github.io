    DHS Launches AI Corps, Calls for 50 AI Experts to Drive Tech Transformation The Department of Homeland Security (DHS) is initiating a “hiring sprint” to assemble a team of 50 artificial intelligence (AI) experts for its newly established AI Corps. The AI Corps, modeled after the U.S. Digital Service, aims to leverage AI technology across various DHS operations. Experts in the AI Corps will contribute to countering illegal drug activities, combating child exploitation, providing immigration services, ensuring national security, protecting critical infrastructure, and enhancing cybersecurity. This move aligns with President Joe Biden’s AI executive order and demonstrates the government’s commitment to hiring AI talent to drive technological advancements in the federal sector. DHS plans to expedite the hiring process using flexible authorities to secure qualified candidates promptly. The goal is to responsibly harness AI capabilities, mitigate misuse, and advance the critical mission of homeland security.

    DHS’s Initial AI Inventory: Verification Issues Highlighted by Watchdog Report According to a report from the Government Accountability Office (GAO), the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) did not properly certify whether the cybersecurity use cases listed in its AI inventory were genuine examples of artificial intelligence. One specific use case, Automated Scoring and Feedback (AS&F), was incorrectly characterized as AI when it was actually a predictive model for sharing cyber threat information. This raises concerns about the accuracy and reliability of DHS’s AI Use Case Inventory. The GAO faulted DHS for not fully implementing the AI Accountability Framework and recommended expanding the AI review process and ensuring the accuracy of inventory submissions. DHS has agreed to all eight recommendations proposed by the GAO, aiming to promote responsible and accountable use of AI and building public trust in the agency’s AI capabilities.
