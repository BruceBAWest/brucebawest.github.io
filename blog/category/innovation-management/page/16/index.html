    Commerce Department Launches AI Safety Consortium with Over 200 Stakeholders The Department of Commerce has introduced an AI safety consortium consisting of more than 200 companies and organizations. This consortium, part of the National Institute of Standards and Technology’s AI Safety Institute, aims to contribute to the actions outlined in President Joe Biden’s AI executive order. The consortium will focus on creating guidelines for red-teaming, capability evaluations, risk management, safety and security, and watermarking synthetic content. Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo emphasized the importance of integrating the consortium’s work with industry and real-world practices, highlighting the diverse range of participants involved, including Amazon, IBM, Apple, MIT, and AFL-CIO Technology Institute. The establishment of the consortium marks a significant step towards responsible AI development and deployment in the United States.

    Government Intensifies AI Implementation with New TMF Program The Technology Modernization Fund (TMF) has announced its willingness to accept applications from federal agencies with proposals for AI projects, signaling the government’s commitment to accelerating the deployment of AI technology. The TMF program will prioritize AI use cases that can be implemented within 18 months and cost up to $6 million to develop. This initiative follows President Joe Biden’s executive order on AI, which mandated the TMF to prioritize AI projects within 30 days. The administration aims to harness the power of AI for positive impact while simultaneously safeguarding against potential risks. According to Clare Martorana, federal Chief Information Officer and TMF Board Chair, the federal government has an obligation to utilize AI for the greater good while protecting citizens. The TMF presents an opportunity to accelerate AI adoption and stimulate innovation in the public sector. The success of AI in various government applications, such as detecting drug shortages and enhancing cybersecurity, underscores the potential of AI in driving operational efficiencies. There are currently over 1,200 indexed agency AI use cases, as reported by the Government Accountability Office. The TMF program demonstrates the government’s interest in both large-scale AI systems and more manageable implementations. The program also emphasizes speed, encouraging agencies with potential proposals to engage with the TMF program management office.

    TMF Calls for Agency Proposals to Fast-Track AI Deployment across Federal Government The Technology Modernization Fund (TMF) is actively seeking investment opportunities in initiatives that will expedite the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) resources throughout the federal government. The TMF’s program management office has extended an open invitation for agency proposals aimed at advancing the goals set out in President Joe Biden’s executive order. This order pertains to the development of AI in the governmental sector that is “safe, secure, and trustworthy.” The General Services Administration, the home of the TMF PMO, announced that TMF board would prioritize reviews for proposals that request up to $6 million in funding and target projects with a completion timeline of 1.5 years or less. This strategy is a concerted effort to boost the government’s AI usage and unlock innovative potential. Federal agencies have a unique opportunity to contribute to the national effort of integrating AI tools across government operations. By catalyzing AI deployment, TMF seeks to enable rapid, responsive service delivery, enhancing both customer experience and the efficiency of administrative tasks.
