    The Future of Federal AI: Predictive, AI Ops, and Generative AI Take Center Stage Federal agencies are increasingly exploring the use of artificial intelligence (AI) to address a wide range of challenges. There are three key types of AI being utilized: predictive AI, AI ops, and generative AI. Predictive AI is commonly used for chatbots, providing faster and more accurate customer service. AI also plays a significant role in surveillance and identification systems, as well as case management systems for trend analysis and sorting. AI ops involves continuous analysis of data flows to detect anomalies and automatically take corrective measures. Lastly, generative AI, while still evolving, is being cautiously adopted by federal agencies, with careful consideration of language models used and the need for substantial computing resources. Overall, the future of federal AI involves understanding the benefits and challenges, and embracing the rapid development and available solutions.

    The Risk Landscape of ChatGPT in Federal Agencies The use of generative AI tools like ChatGPT in federal agencies has prompted various levels of action and response. While some agencies have implemented policies or temporary bans, others have established secure testing environments or created boards to review potential use cases. Different agencies face unique risks and opportunities when it comes to generative AI. The Biden administration’s executive order on AI discourages outright bans, instead emphasizing limited access, data protection, and preventive measures against misuse. The approaches to generative AI vary across agencies, with considerations such as employee guidance, risk assessment, sandbox environments, and relationships with generative AI providers.
