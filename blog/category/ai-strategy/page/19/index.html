    A Successful AI Pilot Is Not Yet a Durable Institution

    Revised and substantially expanded July 17, 2026, including the subsequent legislative and program record.

    In February 2024, lawmakers supporting the Creating Resources for Every American To Experiment with Artificial Intelligence Act—the CREATE AI Act—were optimistic that Congress would formally establish the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR). At the same time, the National Science Foundation had already launched a pilot under executive direction and existing authorities.

    That combination created an easy but misleading story: the pilot was underway, bipartisan support was growing, and legislation would simply make the program permanent.

    The harder institutional question is what permanent should mean.

    A pilot can demonstrate demand, test operating processes, and mobilize voluntary contributions. It cannot by itself guarantee multi-year infrastructure, stable access for long-running research, independent governance, predictable appropriations, enforceable duties, or continuity across administrations. Those are not administrative details surrounding a technical program. They determine what kind of national capability the program becomes.

    NAIRR needs legislation not merely so that it continues to exist, but so that its public obligations survive the incentives and relationships that made the pilot possible.

    Navigating Emerging Threats: The Role of Modern Security Operations Centers With the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI), cybersecurity practitioners and their security operations centers (SOCs) need to be prepared to tackle evolving threats. Continuous training and certifications are crucial for SOC staff to stay ahead of the sophisticated tactics employed by malicious actors. AI has elevated the capabilities of hackers in cyberthreats such as identity theft and spear phishing attacks. SOC staff must adapt by relying on cyber threat intelligence (CTI) to explore the dark web for hacker discussions and by encouraging user reports of suspicious emails. Additionally, a comprehensive SOC should prioritize communication, vulnerability analysis, and the understanding of modernization efforts and secure software design. By incorporating a blend of human intelligence and technical detection, modern SOCs can effectively combat emerging threats and ensure the security of critical systems and networks.
